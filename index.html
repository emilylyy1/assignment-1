Little Keys — A Musical Playground

Synopsis

Little Keys is a browser-based musical toy designed for toddlers to explore sound and discover music through a simple, colourful keyboard interface. The instrument features large, brightly coloured piano keys, each producing a different musical note when pressed. Every interaction is accompanied by playful visual feedback, creating an immediate connection between the child's actions and the sounds they produce. This encourages curiosity, experimentation and a sense of excitement as children discover how different keys create different sounds.

The project focuses on making music accessible to young children by removing the complexity often found in traditional digital audio instruments. Instead of requiring musical knowledge, reading skills or an understanding of technical controls, Little Keys uses familiar visual cues, simple interactions and responsive feedback to support independent exploration. The design prioritises ease of use, with large interactive elements that are easy to identify and press, while its colourful aesthetic creates an inviting and engaging environment. By combining sound, colour and movement, Little Keys aims to provide a playful introduction to musical creativity, allowing toddlers to experiment freely and feel a sense of achievement through their own discoveries.

Design scenario

Scenario: A musical toy designed for toddlers to explore music in a fun and engaging way.

Prompt: Design a way for toddlers to play music and feel excited about their creations.

The scenario frames success around excitement, curiosity and cause-and-effect rather than technical musical skill. The interface therefore prioritises simple actions, clear feedback and an inviting visual style.

How the final project responds

Little Keys presents a simple, interactive keyboard with large, colourful keys designed to make musical exploration accessible and enjoyable for toddlers. Each key produces a distinct tone and responds with a playful animation when pressed, providing immediate audio and visual feedback that helps children understand the relationship between their actions and the sounds they create. The combination of colour, movement and sound encourages children to experiment with different keys, discover new combinations of notes and develop their curiosity through play.

The instrument supports multiple input methods, allowing children to interact using a mouse, touchscreen or mapped computer keyboard. This flexibility makes the experience more accessible across different devices and interaction styles. The interface avoids complicated controls, excessive instructions and unnecessary navigation, keeping the focus on the keyboard and the act of making music. Large interactive keys make individual sounds easy to identify and select, while the use of distinct colours helps differentiate between notes without relying entirely on written labels.

The design also emphasises immediate feedback and creative freedom. Rather than guiding children towards a specific musical outcome, Little Keys allows them to explore sounds at their own pace and learn through trial and error. The responsive animations reinforce each interaction, making the instrument feel engaging and rewarding to use. By combining a straightforward interaction model with a playful visual design, the project responds to the design scenario by creating an approachable musical experience that encourages toddlers to experiment, express themselves and feel excited about their own musical discoveries.

Project documentation

Core interactive features

Little Keys is designed around one simple action: make a sound and see an immediate response. This keeps the instrument approachable for toddlers, who may not be able to read instructions or understand conventional music software.

1. Play a note by clicking or tapping

Each of the eight large keys is an HTML button linked to a musical pitch. Clicking or tapping a key plays its note. The keys are large and separated by visible gaps to make them easier to select on a touchscreen or with a mouse. This direct action-and-response pattern supports the scenario's focus on exploration and cause and effect.

2. Computer keyboard input

The A, S, D, F, G, H, J and K keys trigger the eight corresponding notes. This gives users another way to play and makes it possible to experiment with short sequences. The letter mapping is also shown on each piano key as a reminder. The instrument remains usable with the on-screen keys, so the physical keyboard is optional.

3. Audio and visual feedback

When a note is played, the key briefly moves down and changes its shadow to create a pressed effect. A live status message announces the note name, while the generated tone provides audio feedback. Combining sound, movement, colour and labels makes the result of an action easier to notice and helps connect the user's action with the outcome.

4. Extended technique — mouse movement

The extended technique lets the user play by moving the mouse across the piano keys, rather than needing to click each one. A JavaScript mousemove listener checks which key the pointer is over and plays that key when the pointer enters it. The script remembers the last key entered, so moving within the same key does not repeatedly trigger the same note. When the pointer leaves the keyboard, that remembered key resets. This encourages free-form exploration and lets users create a sequence by sweeping across the colours. Click, tap and A–K keyboard controls remain available as alternatives. Technical reference: MDN — mousemove event.

5. Sound generation in the browser

Little Keys creates tones with the Web Audio API instead of loading prerecorded audio files. JavaScript creates an oscillator for the tone and a gain node to shape its volume. Each key's note value changes the oscillator frequency, while a short fade makes the sound start and finish more smoothly. The audio context is created after a user action to work with browser audio restrictions. Reference: MDN — Web Audio API.

6. Sound toggle and surprise button

The sound toggle turns audio on or off while keeping the keys interactive; this is useful when a caregiver needs to silence the instrument. The “Surprise me” button randomly selects one of the eight keys and plays it, giving users a low-pressure way to discover different notes. It uses JavaScript's Math.random() method. References: MDN — Math.random().

Design system and visual decisions

Colour, shape and typography

The design uses a soft pastel palette of pink, peach, yellow, green, blue and purple. Each note has a distinct colour and a simple symbol, with the note name and computer-key label also visible. This means colour is not the only way to distinguish keys. Rounded corners, soft shadows and the Nunito typeface create a friendly, toy-like appearance that supports the toddler-focused scenario without adding complex decoration to the main interaction.

Layout and responsive behaviour

The keyboard uses CSS Grid so the keys remain aligned and evenly spaced. On wider screens, all eight keys sit in one row. Below 840px, the layout changes to four columns across two rows, giving each key more space on smaller devices. At widths of 1200px and above, the play area and keys can grow while remaining centred; above 1600px, a maximum width prevents the interface from stretching too far. A further breakpoint below 480px reduces key height and simplifies the instruction row. These rules are implemented in instrument.css using CSS media queries. Reference: MDN — using media queries.

Accessibility and ease of use

The keys and controls use semantic HTML buttons, descriptive accessible labels and visible keyboard-focus outlines. Text and symbols accompany the key colours, and the live status region announces interaction feedback. The page does not play audio automatically when it loads. CSS also respects the user's reduced-motion preference by removing transitions. These decisions support a simple interface for children while helping adults and users with different access needs operate it.

Assignment 1 — System Design Document

Assignment 1 explored the design scenario of creating a musical toy that allows toddlers to explore music in a fun and engaging way. The project focused on understanding how a browser-based musical instrument could encourage curiosity and excitement without requiring musical knowledge or reading skills.

Research examined existing musical toys and digital instruments, including the Meowsic cat piano, virtual synthesizers and children's music games. Through this analysis, I explored how interface simplicity, colourful visuals, familiar shapes and immediate audio feedback could make musical interaction more accessible to young children.

The proposed design was a simplified keyboard featuring large, brightly coloured keys, each producing a different synthesiser sound when pressed. The overall aim was to create an intuitive, playful experience where toddlers could experiment with sounds independently and feel excited about their musical discoveries.

Assignment 2 — Parallel Prototypes

Assignment 2 focused on developing three parallel prototypes of a browser-based audio instrument to explore how different interface layouts influence user interaction. The project included a 3 × 3 grid of sound pads, a vertical layout with larger buttons, and a horizontal keyboard-style layout.

Each prototype allowed users to trigger sounds through simple button interactions, while the arrangement, size and organisation of the controls varied. This process helped me explore how interface design could affect usability, accessibility and the overall experience of playing a digital instrument.

Through these experiments, I explored the importance of clear visual organisation, consistent interactions and immediate audio feedback. The horizontal keyboard-style prototype became a useful direction for further development, as its familiar layout provided a simple and intuitive way to interact with sounds. These explorations informed the development of Little Keys in Assignment 3, where the focus shifted towards creating an engaging musical toy specifically designed for toddlers.
